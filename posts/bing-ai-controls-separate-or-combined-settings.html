Bing AI Controls: Separate or Combined Settings

Comparing how publishers can handle Bing's split settings for training and grounding.

26 November 2026

Website owners face a distinct policy choice when Microsoft Bing separates its AI permissions. You must decide whether to let your site data train upcoming language models or just permit current search retrieval. Treating these two functions as a single switch can mean giving away broader asset rights than intended. Examining the available comparison points helps clarify how different site strategies align with these choices.

The Combined Approach

Allowing both model training and grounding under one policy keeps administrative overhead low. This uniform route suits smaller publishing operations with limited resources to monitor every backend setting change. When a team lacks dedicated technical staff to audit log files, a single global rule prevents configuration drift.

The downside is a lack of nuance. By keeping everything open or closed together, you forfeit the ability to benefit from current search visibility while protecting proprietary archives from future machine learning digestion. Sites that rely heavily on referral traffic often lean toward this simplicity, accepting the trade-off to maintain standard indexing status without extra oversight.

The Segmented Approach

Splitting the permissions lets you participate in generative search citations while restricting deep model training. This granular posture fits established content networks, news publishers, and technical documentation sites that produce unique intellectual property. These organizations need their pages read for immediate user answers but want to withhold their text from teaching future artificial intelligence systems.

Implementing this split requires careful monitoring of server logs and crawler behavior. Teams must verify that search engines respect the boundary between retrieval actions and ingestion routines. If the directives fail, the intended protection evaporates. This path suits organizations with active SEO oversight or technical departments capable of tracking bot requests.

Choosing the Right Path

Selecting between these methods depends on your organization's risk tolerance regarding data usage and technical capacity. Teams focused purely on maximizing immediate visibility often choose the simpler path, whereas those guarding specialized assets require distinct boundaries.
