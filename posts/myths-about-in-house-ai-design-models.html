Myths About In-House AI Design Models

Examine common misconceptions surrounding proprietary visual generators and platform-trained intelligence.

29 October 2026

The shift toward proprietary visual generation software has changed how marketing teams evaluate creative assets. Canva Craft represents a major pivot from relying solely on third-party APIs to deploying an in-house model. Yet, leadership teams often approach these native platforms with inherited assumptions about how they function and what they can achieve. Understanding the reality behind these beliefs helps prevent wasted effort and poor creative workflows.

Myth: All visual AI models use the exact same training sources

Many assume that every text-to-image software draws from a shared, chaotic web scrape of public internet imagery. People fear unpredictable copyright complications and stylistic inconsistency because they believe these systems consume random data indiscriminately. In reality, proprietary platforms often curate specific datasets tailored to business communications. For instance, Canva Craft was trained specifically on millions of human-made templates from its own ecosystem, ranging from structured presentations to marketing menus. This targeted curation shapes how the model understands layout, visual hierarchy, and professional context rather than mere artistic flair.

Myth: Single-stage rendering handles complex layouts just fine

A common belief holds that generating an image is a single-pass process where the software spits out a complete picture instantly. Teams often expect traditional prompt boxes to handle intricate marketing requests, such as posters with exact text placement or multi-element infographics, without breaking a sweat. When these single-stage systems misspell words or misplace objects, operators blame AI as a whole. The introduction of two-stage planning architectures changes this dynamic entirely. By mapping out a layout before rendering pixels, modern models attempt to solve the structural failures that plague older, faster generators.

Myth: Resolution and aspect ratios are universal limitations

Another widespread misconception is that generated images are locked into square dimensions and low pixel counts suitable only for casual chat interfaces. Businesses frequently dismiss AI design tools as toys because early iterations struggled to produce assets sharp enough for print or flexible enough for varied social media formats. Modern deployments challenge this notion by supporting higher resolution outputs and dozens of aspect ratios natively. When software accommodates wide banners, vertical stories, and standard print dimensions without losing fidelity, the technical bottleneck shifts from hardware capability to user imagination.
